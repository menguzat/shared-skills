# Online research synthesis — Turkish digital copy

Research basis: current TDK guidance, MEB electronic-content criteria, Google Material UX writing, Google Technical Writing, and public LYF.lab communication samples.

## Stable convergence across sources

Several independent sources converge on the same practical system:

### 1. Clarity before formality
Digital text should help a user complete a task or recover information quickly.

### 2. Concision without robotic language
Shorter text is easier to scan, but overcompression can remove conditions or context.

### 3. One idea / one unit
Long sentences containing multiple independent thoughts are easier to understand when separated.

### 4. Scan architecture
Readers often scan headings, first sentences, labels, and emphasized items before committing to paragraphs.

### 5. Terminology consistency
UI/product writing should maintain preferred terms instead of introducing stylistic synonyms.

### 6. Audience determines tone
Online language can be less formal than print while still being grammatically correct and professionally credible.

### 7. Current orthography still matters
Digital informality is not a license for random spelling/punctuation.

## Turkish-specific adaptation

Do not import English "plain language" prescriptions mechanically.

Turkish differs structurally:
- subject omission is normal;
- suffixes can encode relations English expresses with separate words;
- participles can compact clauses, but can also overload them;
- passive voice is sometimes more natural than an English-centered active-voice rule would suggest;
- word order carries emphasis;
- repeated explicit pronouns can sound translated.

Therefore "plain Turkish" means **simple relationships**, not necessarily English-like short SVO sentences.

## Web quality heuristics

### Page
- page purpose visible quickly;
- first screen identifies category or proposition;
- main CTA specific;
- evidence not buried.

### Section
- one primary purpose;
- heading says what it contains;
- first sentence carries useful information;
- paragraph length supports scanning.

### Sentence
- one dominant claim;
- concrete verb;
- limited unresolved modifiers;
- no semantic duplication.

### Word
- common and precise;
- no decorative foreign term without purpose;
- no obscure purism;
- consistent product terminology.

## Brand writing

Digital brand copy can break neutral-writing conventions when:
- the user is not performing a high-risk task;
- the meaning remains recoverable immediately;
- rhetoric is not used to hide missing facts.

This is the space where `LYF-BOLD` operates.
