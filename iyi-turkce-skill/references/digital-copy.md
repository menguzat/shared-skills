# Digital copy: Turkish web writing

## Reader behavior

Assume:
- scanning before reading;
- mobile access;
- interrupted attention;
- comparison across tabs;
- task-driven reading.

Therefore write for retrieval, not literary suspense.

## Section architecture

A strong section often has:
1. proposition;
2. proof/detail;
3. consequence/action.

Do not hide the point in the fourth paragraph.

## Front-loading

Place differentiating nouns and verbs early.

Weak:
`Daha iyi bir gelecek için geliştirdiğimiz yaklaşım`

Stronger:
`Kenevir lifini tarladan kumaşa izliyoruz`

## Headings

A heading is navigation.

It should work in:
- page outline;
- search result context;
- screen reader heading list;
- skim reading.

Poetic headings need nearby explanatory copy.

## Paragraph density

Prefer visually separable units.

Do not make every sentence a separate paragraph unless the intended voice is highly fragmentary.

## Lists

Use lists for:
- comparable attributes;
- steps;
- requirements;
- options.

Do not convert connected argument into bullets just to look "digital".

## CTA

CTA = action + useful destination/outcome.

Prefer:
- `Numune iste`
- `Teknik föyü indir`
- `Üretim sürecini incele`

Avoid generic:
- `Keşfet`;
- `Başla`;
- `Devam`;
unless context makes outcome obvious.

## Link language

Avoid `buraya tıklayın`.
Link the meaningful noun/action.

## Above the fold

A hero should usually answer:
- What is this?
- Why is it distinct?
- What can I do next?

A brand can deliberately create intrigue, but intrigue must not replace category comprehension.

## Conversion copy

Before persuasion, resolve:
- fit;
- price/price logic;
- availability;
- process;
- lead time;
- risk;
- returns/next step;
- evidence.

Copy cannot compensate for missing commercial information.

## SEO

Natural Turkish first.
Use topic terms in meaningful places:
- title;
- H1;
- intro;
- descriptive headings.

Do not distort suffixes or repeat exact phrases unnaturally.
