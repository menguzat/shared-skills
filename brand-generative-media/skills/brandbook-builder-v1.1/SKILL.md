# Brandbook Builder

## Core principle

A brandbook is not a decorated strategy deck and not a logo manual. It is a **brand operating system**: a set of strategic, verbal, visual, behavioral, and production rules that lets different people and tools create new artifacts without losing identity.

The objective is not “make it look branded.” The objective is:

> A designer, developer, copywriter, photographer, architect, printer, social editor, event producer, or AI agent should be able to create something that has never existed before — and still have it unmistakably belong to the same brand.

Treat the logo as one component. In many brands, typography, photography, spatial behavior, language, or product experience carries more identity than the mark.

---

# 1. Choose the operating mode

## DISCOVERY
Use when the brand is under-defined. Read available material first; then ask only unresolved questions.

Output: missing decisions, evidence request, contradictions, working hypotheses.

Reference: `references/discovery-and-evidence.md`

## SOURCE AUDIT
Use when source material exists: decks, PDFs, sites, product catalogs, social accounts, old brandbooks, legal/company docs, founder notes, screenshots, imagery.

Separate every conclusion into:
- **FACT** — directly supported.
- **CURRENT STATE** — observable now.
- **INTENT** — stated future direction.
- **INFERENCE** — reasoned conclusion.
- **PROPOSAL** — new recommendation.

Never turn a proposal into invented history.

## STRATEGY
Create positioning, brand essence, first-impression sequence, values-as-behaviors, personality, proof, audience, and brand architecture.

References:
- `references/source-dossier-and-strategy.md`
- `references/brand-architecture.md`

## VISUAL TERRITORY
Create 3–5 genuinely different design directions before converging. A different palette on the same layout is not a new territory.

Reference: `references/visual-territories-and-taste.md`

## BRAND SYSTEM
Formalize approved territory into logo, typography, color, grid, graphic grammar, iconography, photography, motion, digital UI, physical applications, social, naming, and governance.

References:
- `references/logo-type-color-grid.md`
- `references/photography-motion-material.md`
- `references/digital-physical-applications.md`

## BRAND BOOK PRODUCTION
Turn the system into a coherent book: page architecture, pacing, copy, specs, mockups, and application evidence.

Reference: `references/brandbook-architecture-and-production.md`

## AUDIT / REFINE
Audit an existing identity for generic design, incoherence, weak proof, unusable rules, bad language, and missing applications. Preserve what works; avoid redesign for redesign’s sake.

Reference: `references/qa-and-audit-rubric.md`

# 2. Mandatory workflow

Unless the user explicitly changes it:

1. Read all available sources.
2. Build a source dossier.
3. Identify contradictions and unknowns.
4. Resolve brand architecture before logo proliferation.
5. Write the strategic core.
6. Write verbal identity in the brand’s primary language **natively**, not by translation.
7. Define 3–5 visual territories with written design contracts.
8. Generate territory boards.
9. Run anti-AI-slop / taste audit.
10. User selects or combines a direction deliberately.
11. Expand the chosen territory into system boards.
12. Develop and test logo only now, unless an official logo already exists.
13. Generate page-level brandbook mockups.
14. Test the identity across real touchpoints.
15. Convert visual intent into deterministic production specifications.
16. Run final QA and governance checks.

Do not skip from “three adjectives” directly to a logo.

# 3. Evidence before identity

Request or inspect, when relevant:

- exact brand and legal names
- founder/origin story
- current revenue model
- actual products/services
- future roadmap
- audiences and buying contexts
- competitors and substitutes
- physical places and materials
- existing websites and apps
- social channels
- logo masters
- packaging / signage / decks / print
- real photography
- reviews / testimonials
- partnerships / vendors / sub-brands
- claims, certifications, measurements
- legal/regulatory constraints
- internal culture and operating model

Do not ask for information already visible in sources.

When the brand has a real history, place, material, production method, people, archive, or working process, treat those as higher-value identity inputs than generic aesthetic references.

# 4. Brand source dossier

Before visual design, distill:

## REALITY
What exists and is verifiable.

## AMBITION
What the brand wants to become.

## AUDIENCE
Who needs it and why.

## POSITION
Why this over alternatives.

## TENSION
Productive contrasts such as:
- institution × play
- local × international
- craft × technology
- field × laboratory
- premium × raw
- structure × improvisation
- history × present
- physical × digital

Do not flatten useful tension into “balanced.” Strong identity often lives in the friction.

## PROOF
What substantiates the promise.

## FIRST-IMPRESSION SEQUENCE
Define what should happen perceptually in order. Example:
1. curiosity
2. desire
3. recognition of people/culture
4. understanding
5. explanation/history

Not every brand should explain itself in the hero.

# 5. Brand architecture

Map the system explicitly:

- masterbrand
- legal company
- product brands
- endorsed brands
- programs
- projects
- collections
- locations
- services
- marketplace vendors
- partnerships
- temporary campaigns

For every entity define:

- **ROLE** — what it is
- **RELATIONSHIP** — how it connects
- **NAMING** — how it is named
- **VISUAL INDEPENDENCE** — how far it may diverge
- **ENDORSEMENT** — whether/how the masterbrand appears

Do not confuse the founders’ other companies, strategic partners, vendors, and owned sub-brands.

Reference: `references/brand-architecture.md`

---

# 6. Brand essence: use behavior, not adjective clouds

For each major brand layer define:

- ROLE
- PURPOSE
- FEATURE
- BENEFIT
- VALUE
- CHARACTER
- ESSENCE
- PROOF

For values, use:

### WE BELIEVE
The principle.

### THEREFORE WE DO
Observable behavior.

### THEREFORE WE DO NOT
Constraint.

Example:

**CURIOUS** is not “innovative.” It may mean: tests alternatives, asks questions, publishes findings, changes tools when a better path appears. It does not mean trend-chasing.

Always create a negative identity:

### THE BRAND IS / THE BRAND IS NOT

Examples:
- raw / not careless
- premium / not luxury theatre
- playful / not childish
- historical / not retro cosplay
- ecological / not eco-market cliché
- technical / not intimidating
- experimental / not chaotic

---

# 7. Verbal identity must be written natively

If Turkish is primary, write Turkish from scratch. Do not draft English and translate it.

Define tone as behavior:

### KISA KONUŞURUZ
Bir cümle yetiyorsa üç cümle kurmayız.

### SOMUT KONUŞURUZ
Soyut vaat yerine yapılan işi söyleriz.

### TEKNİK TERİMDEN KORKMAYIZ
Ama terimi üstünlük kurmak için kullanmayız.

### İDDİAYI KANITIN ÖNÜNE KOYMAYIZ
Gösterilemeyen şeyi büyütmeyiz.

Always define:
- USE vocabulary
- AVOID vocabulary
- NEVER claims / constructions
- headline examples
- body examples
- microcopy examples
- error / support tone if digital
- naming rules

Default anti-hype scan:
- future-proof / future-ready
- unlock your potential
- revolutionary
- game-changing
- seamless
- empower
- elevate
- disruptive
- innovative ecosystem
- transformative journey
- world-class

Use concrete verbs and nouns instead.

Reference: `references/verbal-identity-and-native-copy.md`

# 8. Visual territory before logo

Create a visual equation derived from meaning, for example:

- FIELD × DOCUMENT × OBJECT × CULTURE
- MEMORY × PRESENT BEHAVIOR × REAL LIFE
- CRAFT × DATA × MOVEMENT
- INSTITUTION × PLAY

Each layer must perform a different identity function.

For each proposed territory write a **design contract** before generating images:

- CORE IDEA
- TYPE STANCE
- COLOR PHILOSOPHY
- GRID / COMPOSITION
- PHOTOGRAPHY
- GRAPHIC DEVICES
- MATERIAL
- MOTION
- SIGNATURE DETAIL
- NEVER

Template: `templates/direction-contract-template.md`

Generate 3–5 directions that differ structurally, not cosmetically.

# 9. Image generation is an art-direction instrument

Use image generation in four phases:

## 9.1 EXPLORE
Broad territory boards. Judge world, hierarchy, typography character, photography, material, application feel.

## 9.2 EXPAND
After direction selection, generate focused boards: logo, type, color, photography, social, signage, packaging, web, merch, motion frames.

## 9.3 SPECIFY
Generate individual brandbook pages/spreads instead of endlessly requesting mega-collages.

## 9.4 PROVE
Generate realistic applications in actual contexts and production methods.

Important:
- Generated image text is **not final typesetting**.
- For exploration, approximate text is acceptable.
- For approval, judge hierarchy and art direction, not spelling.
- Final production must be rebuilt in deterministic layout software/code when exact text, fonts, dimensions, or logo geometry matter.

Full playbook: `references/image-generation-playbook.md`
Prompts: `prompts/`

# 10. Official logo rule

If the user supplies an official logo, always use it.

Do not:
- approximate it with text
- redraw it without permission
- substitute a related brand mark
- invent a new symbol in a page that is not explicitly logo exploration
- recolor outside defined rules

If only raster artwork exists, use it for mockups and separately flag the need for a vector production master.

When no logo exists, prioritize wordmark authorship before generic symbols.

Reference: `references/logo-type-color-grid.md`

# 11. Anti-AI-slop gate

Generated design has statistical tells. Detect them deliberately.

Default flags:
- purple→blue SaaS gradient with no rationale
- glassmorphism
- every section made of rounded cards
- giant pill CTAs everywhere
- generic bento layout
- generic thin icon inside rounded square
- arbitrary AI network / brain / sparkle imagery
- meaningless charts and fake metrics
- equal-weight feature grids
- huge centered SaaS hero by reflex
- decorative “SECTION 01” micro-label spam
- random 3D blobs
- superficial paper grain added to simulate taste
- one serif italic word inside every sans headline
- same fashionable font stack on unrelated brands

Ask of each element:

> Is this a decision or a default?

If nobody can explain why the gradient, corner radius, icon, serif, animation, color, or texture exists, remove or reconsider it.

A clean anti-slop pass is necessary but insufficient. Also ask:
1. **Justified** — does every move serve the direction?
2. **Coherent** — do type, palette, layout, image and material reinforce one idea?
3. **Not a rerun** — did the system avoid the same “safe tasteful” answer used on unrelated projects?

Reference: `references/visual-territories-and-taste.md`

# 12. Logo development

Test routes:
1. wordmark
2. custom typographic intervention
3. compact derivative mark
4. seal/stamp only when historically or functionally earned

A secondary stamp must not accidentally become the entire identity.

Test every logo for:
- distance signage
- embroidery
- stencil
- one-color print
- photocopy
- tiny digital header
- partner lockup
- merch desirability
- 5–10 year durability
- unintended category drift (sports club, AI startup, fashion, wellness, etc.)

Useful question:

> Would someone wear this without already knowing the brand?

---

# 13. Typography, color, graphic language

Typography may carry more identity than the logo.

Define only voices that have jobs:
- DISPLAY
- TEXT / UTILITY
- MONO when functional
- ACCENT only when earned

Define Turkish glyph coverage and actual hierarchy.

Color must be role-based, not merely swatches:
- FOUNDATION
- BRAND
- SIGNAL
- FUNCTION
- SEASONAL / PROGRAM

Derive color from real brand inputs where possible: place, material, product, history, production, category contrast.

Graphic devices are a vocabulary, not a collage. Potential tools:
- grid
- index / numbering
- arrows
- circles
- rules
- coordinates
- crop behavior
- diagrams
- handwriting
- field geometry
- provenance labels
- stamps
- technical readouts

Use 1–3 per composition. Never deploy the entire toolbox everywhere.

---

# 14. Photography: show brand behavior

Define:
- subject
- distance
- camera behavior
- light
- movement
- acceptable imperfection
- edit / grain / color

Useful two-layer model:

### SCALE
Architecture, infrastructure, landscape, capability.

### SOUL
People, gestures, rituals, material, action, daily life.

Wide architecture alone becomes real estate. Close lifestyle alone hides capability. Use both intentionally.

Photographs should often show the brand **doing** its proposition rather than symbolizing it.

Reference: `references/photography-motion-material.md`

---

# 15. Digital and physical translation

Do not literally paste the print brandbook onto the website.

Translate:
- hierarchy
- spacing philosophy
- type roles
- color roles
- graphic grammar
- image direction
- tone

into usable digital behavior.

When digital product is part of the brand, define design tokens for color, spacing, typography, radii, border, state, motion and breakpoints.

Physical tests should use plausible:
- material
- printing method
- mounting
- scale
- lighting
- wear
- architecture

Do not brand every surface.

Reference: `references/digital-physical-applications.md`

---

# 16. Brandbook pacing

Do not make every page equally dense.

Alternate:
- **DENSE** strategy/specification
- **BREATHING** manifesto
- **VISUAL** photography/application
- **TECHNICAL** logo/grid/type

One page, one primary idea.

Avoid 30 specification sheets and avoid 30 moodboards. A useful brandbook oscillates between meaning, rule, evidence, and inspiration.

Reference: `references/brandbook-architecture-and-production.md`

---

# 17. Iteration protocol

After every major generation or design pass, classify:

## KEEP
Strong and aligned.

## DEVELOP
Promising but unresolved.

## REMOVE
Generic, contradictory or unnecessary.

## MISSING
System component not yet designed.

Regenerate only what needs change. Do not throw away the entire identity after each critique.

---

# 18. Final QA

Before completion, run `references/qa-and-audit-rubric.md`.

Minimum gate:

### Strategy
- positioning specific
- proof separated from aspiration
- architecture clear
- negative identity defined

### Verbal
- primary language native
- no translated syntax
- no hype defaults
- examples exist

### Visual
- official logo correct
- typography has jobs
- color has roles
- photography is specific
- application tests prove flexibility
- system is not dependent on one hero mockup

### Operational
- fixed vs flexible defined
- co-branding covered
- digital/physical rules covered where needed
- governance/versioning defined

# 20. Final principle

Do not ask only:

> What should this brand look like?

Ask:

> What does this brand need to make possible repeatedly?

Build the visual, verbal, behavioral, and production system that makes those outcomes recognizable.
