# Brand Architecture

## Why architecture precedes logos

Identity systems become expensive and incoherent when every activity receives a new logo before its role is clear.

## Entity classes

### Masterbrand
Primary reputation holder.

### Product/service line
A commercial offer inside the masterbrand.

### Program
A recurring format or activity; often does not require a standalone brand.

### Project/pilot
Temporary or experimental work.

### Endorsed brand
Has its own name/world but receives trust from the masterbrand.

### Independent partner
Related operationally but not owned.

### Vendor/producer
Appears inside a marketplace or distribution context; retains its own identity.

### Location
May require wayfinding naming but not a new corporate identity.

## Architecture test

A new standalone identity is justified only when several of these materially differ:
- audience
- value proposition
- distribution
- cultural world
- product system
- reputation risk
- ownership

A new category alone is not enough.

## Per-entity schema

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Role | What is it? |
| Owner | Who owns/controls it? |
| Audience | Who is it for? |
| Promise | What does it promise? |
| Naming | How is it named? |
| Mark | Which mark appears? |
| Independence | Low / medium / high |
| Endorsement | None / endorsed / masterbrand-led |
| Lifetime | Temporary / recurring / permanent |

## Co-branding

Define:
- dominance
- clearspace
- relative size
- order
- “in partnership with” language
- event/sponsor exceptions

Never infer legal ownership from shared founders, shared projects, or a partner logo on a website.
