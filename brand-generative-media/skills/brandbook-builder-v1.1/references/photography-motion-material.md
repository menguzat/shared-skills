# Photography, Motion & Material

## Photography is evidence

The strongest brand photography often documents the actual proposition:
- people doing the work
- product in use
- material/process
- place/infrastructure
- rituals/community
- consequences/results

## Art-direction schema

### SUBJECT
Who/what appears?

### DISTANCE
Wide / human / close / macro.

### CAMERA
Documentary, compact, 35mm, architectural, studio, phone-native, etc.

### LIGHT
Natural, hard noon, flash, overcast, night, studio.

### MOVEMENT
Still, motion blur, handheld, tripod, tracking.

### IMPERFECTION
Acceptable crop, grain, flash falloff, blur, occlusion.

### EDIT
Contrast, saturation, black-and-white, warmth, grain.

## Scale × soul

Use wide/drone/architecture to explain **scale and possibility**.
Use close/human/action to explain **soul and belonging**.

A complete image system usually needs both.

## Synthetic-image caution

If AI imagery is used because real photography does not yet exist:
- define it as art-direction prototype
- avoid fabricating documentary evidence
- do not imply generated people are actual employees/customers/producers
- plan a real shoot list for final identity where authenticity matters

## Motion

Motion should be a behavioral extension:
- playful → responsive/kinetic
- technical → update/state/sequence
- archival → reveal/layer
- premium → restrained pacing

Avoid floating decorative animation with no semantic role.

## Material

Specify physical behavior:
- paper stock
- coating/no coating
- embroidery
- screen print
- stencil
- vinyl
- metal
- paint
- fabric patch
- emboss/deboss
- environmental wear

A mockup is stronger when the production method is plausible.
