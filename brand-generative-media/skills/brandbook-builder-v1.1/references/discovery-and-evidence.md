# Discovery & Evidence

## Principle

Discovery is not a questionnaire ritual. Its purpose is to reduce uncertainty before design decisions are made.

Read existing sources first. Ask only questions whose answers would materially change strategy, architecture, copy, or identity.

## Minimum evidence request

### Business reality
- Exact brand and legal names
- Current products/services
- Revenue-driving activities
- Geographic markets
- Distribution/sales model
- Existing customer groups
- Pricing position
- Planned products/markets

### Origin and people
- Founder story
- Real reason the organization exists
- Meaningful historical events
- Relevant team members
- Whether founder visibility matters

### Existing expression
- Logo masters
- Typography licenses/files available internally
- Color values
- Websites/apps
- Social accounts
- Presentations
- Packaging
- Signage
- Photography/video archive
- Print/editorial material

### Proof
- Certifications
- Measurements
- Product tests
- Customer reviews
- Published case studies
- Provenance/traceability data
- Awards, partnerships, press only if materially relevant

### Constraints
- Trademark status
- Legal/regulatory claims
- Accessibility obligations
- Languages
- Production methods
- Budget/implementation capability

## High-value discovery questions

### Reality
1. What do you actually do today?
2. What pays the bills today?
3. What work best represents where you are going?
4. What should not define you even if it is currently visible?

### Origin
5. Why did this start?
6. What part of the story is real enough that it should never need to be invented later?
7. Is there a place, process, material, person, archive, or ritual that belongs uniquely to the brand?

### Ambition
8. What should be true in 3–5 years?
9. Which categories might be added?
10. What must still feel true after growth?

### Audience
11. Who is the real customer/user/participant today?
12. What are they trying to accomplish?
13. What do they fear or doubt before choosing you?
14. What would make them tell another person about the brand?

### Positioning
15. What does the customer choose if they do not choose you?
16. What is actually different rather than merely phrased differently?
17. Which claim can you prove?

### Personality
18. If the brand were a competent person in a room, how would it behave?
19. What behavior would embarrass the brand?
20. Which competitor aesthetic would be disastrous even if commercially successful?

### Expression
21. What should a person feel before they understand the brand?
22. What should they understand only after spending time with it?
23. Which physical/digital touchpoint is the identity’s hardest test?

## Evidence discipline

Label internally:
- FACT
- CURRENT STATE
- INTENT
- INFERENCE
- PROPOSAL

When sources conflict, show the conflict. Do not silently reconcile it.

## Stop condition

Discovery is sufficient when the agent can state, without aesthetic language:
- what the brand does
- for whom
- why it is chosen
- what is distinct
- what is provable
- what can change
- what must not change
