# Source Dossier & Strategy

## Brand source dossier

Create this before visual territory work.

### 1. Reality
What exists, with source references.

### 2. Ambition
What the organization wants to become.

### 3. Audience jobs
Use motivations/jobs rather than decorative personas.

### 4. Category and alternatives
What mental shelf the brand sits on now; what it competes against.

### 5. Positioning
A useful structure:

> For [audience/job], [brand] is [category/frame] that [distinct outcome], because [proof/mechanism].

Do not force this sentence into public copy. It is an internal precision tool.

### 6. Productive tensions
Find 2–4 tensions that can generate visual and verbal decisions.

Examples:
- archive × present tense
- infrastructure × hospitality
- discipline × play
- craft × data
- local material × international culture

### 7. Brand idea
One short idea broad enough to survive category expansion, specific enough to reject bad work.

### 8. Promise
What experience/outcome can consistently be expected.

### 9. Proof
Evidence hierarchy:
1. independently verified
2. internally measured
3. source/producer claim, attributed
4. aspiration/hypothesis, labeled

### 10. First-impression hierarchy
Design what is perceived in sequence. Typical useful pattern:
- desire/character first
- relevance second
- understanding third
- history/process later

### 11. Brand principles
Each principle requires observable behavior and anti-behavior.

## Strategy anti-patterns

Avoid:
- “innovative, sustainable, human, bold” with no behavioral definition
- mission statements that simply list sectors
- values that no business decision could violate
- founder mythology replacing customer relevance
- category jargon used as positioning
- invented social impact without measurement
- “premium” as a visual style instead of a price/value system
