# Verbal Identity & Native-Language Copy

## Native-first rule

For the primary brand language, think and write directly in that language. Do not preserve English syntax and replace words.

For Turkish, common translation tells include:
- abstract noun stacks
- excessive “deneyim”, “ekosistem”, “çözüm”, “dönüşüm”
- “X’i mümkün kılan” constructions
- unnecessary possessives
- marketing adjectives where a verb would work
- “gerçek dünya çıktıları” instead of naming the actual output

Prefer verbs and concrete nouns.

Example:

Bad:
> Yapay zekâ destekli yenilikçi üretim deneyimi sunuyoruz.

Better:
> Fikri seçiyor, işi parçalıyor, araçları kuruyor ve ürünü yayına alıyoruz.

## Voice versus tone

Voice is stable character. Tone adapts to situation.

Define voice as 3–5 operating behaviors, not adjectives.

For each:
- principle
- what it sounds like
- what it does not sound like
- headline example
- body example
- microcopy example

## Vocabulary matrix

### USE
Words tied to actual brand behavior.

### USE CAREFULLY
Technical/category words that may need explanation.

### AVOID
Overused, vague, or category-cliché terms.

### NEVER
Unsupported claims, prohibited language, or identity-breaking tone.

## Native copy QA

Read the text aloud. Ask:
- Would a good editor write this without seeing English first?
- Can any abstract noun become a verb?
- Is the sentence longer than the thought?
- Is the claim more ambitious than the evidence?
- Can the brand say the same thing more concretely?
- Is humor natural or performative?
