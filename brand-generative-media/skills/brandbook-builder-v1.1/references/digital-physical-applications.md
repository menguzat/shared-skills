# Digital & Physical Applications

## Digital translation

Do not mimic print. Translate the underlying rules.

Define design tokens where digital product matters:
- color roles
- typography
- spacing
- radius
- border
- elevation
- motion
- breakpoints
- states

Useful principle: tokens reduce arbitrary choice and improve designer/developer communication.

### Digital tests
- responsive logo behavior
- navigation
- type scale
- contrast
- focus/hover/disabled/error/success
- content density
- loading/empty states
- image crops
- accessible motion
- dark/light theme if applicable

A brand system that only shows a hero homepage is incomplete.

## Physical translation

Test identity against real operational surfaces:
- entry signage
- wayfinding
- temporary event signs
- ID/pass
- menu
- uniforms
- merchandise
- vehicles
- packaging
- notice boards
- publication

Ask whether the identity becomes stronger in the physical environment, not merely visible.

## Merch as cultural test

For community/culture brands:
> Would a person wear the object without being paid or already being a fan?

If yes, the mark/type/language may have become a cultural sign rather than promotion.

## Co-branding

Always show at least one real partner/sponsor/vendor context when partnerships are common. A logo that only works alone is under-tested.
