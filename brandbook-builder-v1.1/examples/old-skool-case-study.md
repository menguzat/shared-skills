# Case Study — Old Skool

Source: user-supplied `Old Skool Marka Kimliği — Editable DOCX Import.pdf` (working draft, August 2026).

This is included as a methodological example, not a reusable aesthetic template.

## What made the brief useful

### 1. It started from real history
The place was actually a school. The identity did not need to invent heritage.

The critical strategic move was **not** to make “old school” visual nostalgia the whole brand. The brief reframed school history as a memory layer and made “playground” the present behavior.

### 2. It built a hierarchy of meaning
- PLAYGROUND = current vision/behavior
- SCHOOL MEMORY = historical/cultural layer
- REGENERATION = long-term operating principle

This prevented the identity from becoming either a retro school theme or a generic ecological center.

### 3. It specified first-impression sequence
The desired reaction was closer to:
1. What is this?
2. Interesting people are here.
3. I want to go.
4. Later: it used to be a school and is being re-established.

This is stronger than trying to explain the entire organization above the fold.

### 4. It defined positive and negative identity together
Examples of useful tensions:
- playful, not childish
- raw, not careless
- ecological, not eco-market cliché
- historical, not vintage decoration
- experimental, not chaotic

### 5. It made visual territory layered
The design territory combined:
- school memory: numbering, public graphics, field lines, archive logic
- playground now: bold type, movement, editorial grid, live photography
- real life: food, sport, dogs, pool, caravan, workshop, unfinished work, people

The third layer is important: graphic design frames reality; it does not substitute for it.

### 6. It prioritized identity components correctly
The brief explicitly prioritized:
1. playground idea
2. typography
3. photography/movement
4. logo
5. graphic system
6. social/physical applications

This is a useful antidote to logo-first branding.

### 7. It treated physical experience as a brand test
Wayfinding, field graphics, IDs, menus, timetable, merch and notices all needed to work as one family.

### 8. It used merch as a cultural test
A strong question: would someone wear the sweatshirt even if they did not already know Old Skool?

## What not to universalize

Do not copy:
- school stamps
- track red / notebook blue
- condensed sports type
- paper grain
- “Est. Again.”

Those are earned by this specific project's history. The reusable lesson is the method: find the real source of identity, define the present behavior, then convert the tension into a system.
