# Case Study — HASAT

Methodological summary of an identity/brand-architecture exploration for a cooperative and marketplace.

## Challenge

A broad organization spanning agriculture, culture, technology, community and commerce risked becoming either:
- a generic “sustainable green” brand
- a collection of unrelated sub-brands
- a marketplace that visually erased its producers

## Useful moves

### 1. Separate organization from marketplace role
A cooperative/institution can be the trust source while the marketplace acts as a curated commercial interface.

### 2. Do not confuse adjacent entities
Partner companies, founders' other ventures, project collaborators, and vendor brands are not automatically sub-brands.

### 3. Use proof as part of identity
Instead of “natural / sustainable / healthy” adjective stacks, use:
- producer
- origin
- material
- method
- batch
- documents
- certifications
- measurements

### 4. Let producer identity survive
A marketplace can provide structure, provenance and trust while allowing vendors/producers to retain character.

### 5. Build visual territory from reality
One useful conceptual system was:
- FIELD
- DOCUMENT
- OBJECT
- CULTURE

This made the identity broader than “green agriculture.”

## Reusable lesson

For marketplaces and ecosystems, brand architecture and provenance design can matter more than inventing a highly dominant consumer aesthetic.
