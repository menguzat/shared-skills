# Hemp / Cannabis-Derived Products — 2026 Special Module

## Primary source

`TR-HEMP-2026`: Kenevirden Elde Edilen Ürünlere Dair Yönetmelik, RG 31.01.2026/33154.

The appended official text defines regulated hemp-derived medical products, health products, personal-care products and support products, and includes rules on licensing/notification, tracking and sale.

## Critical verified provisions

From the reproduced official text:
- Article 4 defines cannabinoids to include examples such as THC, CBD, CBN and CBG.
- The definition of hemp-derived support product refers to products used for health-support purposes containing cannabinoids or combinations, with the stated THC limit in the definition.
- The definition of hemp-derived personal-care products refers to cosmetic products containing cannabinoids or combinations for personal-care purposes, with the stated THC limit in the definition.
- Article 5 states that unlicensed health/support products containing hemp-derived components cannot be placed on the market.
- Article 24 routes hemp-derived personal-care products through cosmetics notification/tracking and bars placing them on the market if the required cosmetic notification is not recorded.
- Article 34 states that medical, health, personal-care and support products **within the Regulation’s scope are sold only in pharmacies**; medical and health products are subject to the prescription system described there.

## Do not over-apply this regulation

The regulation’s product definitions matter. Do not mark every product containing the word “hemp/kenevir” as pharmacy-only.

### Usually requires a different route first
Examples that may fall outside this special health/personal-care regime depending on composition and other law:
- hemp fibre textile,
- hemp paper,
- hemp rope,
- hurd/building material,
- hemp seed,
- hemp hearts,
- ordinary seed-derived food,
- hemp seed oil as ordinary food,
- a cosmetic whose only cannabis-related ingredient is seed oil and which does not meet the Regulation’s cannabinoid-based personal-care definition.

These still require compliance with their own sector rules.

## Decision tree

1. Identify material/plant part: seed, seed oil, fibre, hurd, stalk, flower, leaf, extract, distillate.
2. Identify process: pressing, extraction, distillation, fractionation, enrichment, other industrial process.
3. Obtain cannabinoid status if material: THC, THCA, CBD, CBDA, CBG, CBN and relevant combinations.
4. Determine intended use: food, cosmetic/personal care, health support, medicine, textile/general product.
5. Compare facts to the regulation’s definitions.
6. If within regulated medical/health/personal-care/support scope -> `PHARMACY_ONLY` for normal e-commerce.
7. If classification hinges on formula/analysis -> `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and request evidence.

## Website claim triggers

The following should trigger deeper review, not an automatic conclusion:
- CBD / cannabidiol,
- cannabinoid-rich,
- full-spectrum/broad-spectrum,
- CBG/CBN,
- “health support”, “relaxation”, “pain”, “sleep”, “immune”,
- leaf/flower extract,
- cannabis extract without specification.

## Required evidence examples

- full ingredient/INCI list,
- supplier specification,
- cannabinoid panel,
- THC analysis,
- plant-part and extraction-method statement,
- TİTCK status/notification/licence evidence where applicable,
- cultivation/source documentation where required by the regulation or connected rules.

## Reporting rule

If an item is within Article 34 scope, do not recommend deleting claims as a workaround for online sale. The channel restriction follows product status, not merely advertising wording.
