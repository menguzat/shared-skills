# Rhythm and emphasis in Turkish

## Emphasis is not just adjectives

Turkish can emphasize through:
- word order;
- predicate proximity;
- repetition;
- pause/punctuation;
- `bir`;
- pronoun;
- intensifier;
- sentence length.

Use the lightest mechanism that works.

## Predicate proximity

Elements close to the predicate often carry focus.

Do not import English emphasis by adding words such as `gerçekten`, `oldukça`, `özellikle` when syntax can carry focus.

## Short sentence

A short sentence can:
- establish thesis;
- change direction;
- land a consequence.

Too many short sentences produce artificial solemnity.

## Long sentence

Long Turkish sentences are not inherently bad.
They fail when:
- skeleton is lost;
- modifiers stack;
- subject changes unnoticed;
- reader must retain too many unresolved dependencies.

## Punctuation

Use punctuation to expose structure, not decorate rhythm.

A comma can clarify scope, but should not be used to repair a sentence whose grammar is broken.

## Read aloud

For brand copy, read aloud after editing:
- does stress land naturally?
- do repeated suffixes clatter?
- do three slogans in a row sound machine-generated?
- is any phrase only impressive on screen but awkward when spoken?
