# Copywriting system

## Copy is decision support

Persuasion is strongest when the reader can answer:
- Is this for me?
- What is different?
- What evidence supports it?
- What does it cost / require?
- What happens next?
- What risk remains?

## Core structures

### Proposition → mechanism → evidence → action
Best for technical/B2B pages.

### Problem → distinction → mechanism → proof
Best when market category is misunderstood.

### Claim → qualification → evidence
Best for scientific/environmental content.

### Object → property → consequence
Best for product pages.

Do not force AIDA/PAS or any single formula onto every page.

## Benefit language

A benefit is a consequence for a user/system, not a positive adjective.

Feature:
`Değiştirilebilir manşet.`

Benefit:
`Aşınan manşet değiştirilebilir; tüm gömleği yenilemek gerekmez.`

Only use the consequence if product design truly supports it.

## Objections

Answer material objections close to the decision point:
- sizing;
- compatibility;
- origin;
- care;
- delivery;
- lead time;
- MOQ;
- certification;
- returns.

Do not hide them under FAQ when they determine purchase eligibility.

## Specificity

Specificity can be:
- number;
- place;
- material;
- process;
- timeframe;
- test;
- named limitation;
- concrete comparison.

Specificity without truth is worse than generic language.

## Restraint

Do not put a punchline after every paragraph.
Strong copy needs neutral connective tissue.
