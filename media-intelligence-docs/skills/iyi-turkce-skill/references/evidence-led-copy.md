# Evidence-led copy

## Rule

Strong language requires strong evidence.

## Claim classes

### Factual
`Kumaş %55 kenevir içerir.`

Requires direct data.

### Comparative
`Daha hafif.`
Compared with what? Under what method?

### Superlative
`En dayanıklı`.
Requires defined comparison universe and evidence.

### Environmental
`Daha düşük karbon`.
Requires boundary, method, comparison, period, data.

### Health/performance
High-risk. Require qualified evidence and legal review where relevant.

### Aspirational
`Daha döngüsel bir sistem kurmayı hedefliyoruz.`
Clearly framed as goal, not achieved outcome.

## Evidence rewrite ladder

Unsupported:
`Çevre dostu üretim`

Process statement:
`Boyamada su kullanımını azaltmaya yönelik kapalı devre sistemi test ediyoruz.`

Measured statement:
`Pilot süreçte su tüketimi X'ten Y'ye düştü.`

Verified statement:
`[Test/certification/source] ...`

Only climb as high as evidence permits.

## Limits improve trust

When meaningful, state:
- what was measured;
- what was not;
- pilot vs. production;
- estimate vs. measured value;
- date/version.

Bold brands can state limits without sounding weak.
