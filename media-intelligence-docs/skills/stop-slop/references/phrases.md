# Phrases to Remove

## The "AI Tell" Word List

**Strictly Ban** these words. They are red flags for AI generation.

| Word | Why to ban it | Replacement |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| **Delve** | The #1 AI cliché. | Explore, investigate, look into |
| **Tapestry** | Overused metaphor. | Mix, variety, collection |
| **Testament** | Melodramatic. | Proof, evidence, example |
| **Landscape** | Meaningless filler. | Situation, field, context |
| **Foster** | Vague corporate-speak. | Build, create, encourage |
| **Spearhead** | Cliché action verb. | Lead, direct |
| **Underscore** | Overused emphasis. | Highlight, show |
| **Nuance** | Often used to hide lack of detail. | Detail, subtlety |

## Throat-Clearing Openers

Remove these announcement phrases. State the content directly.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"

## Robotic Transitions

AI loves these. **Cut them entirely.**

- **"In conclusion,"** (Just stop writing. Or say "The result was...")
- **"It is important to note,"** (If it's important, just say it.)
- **"It is worth noting,"** (Same as above.)
- **"Furthermore,"** (Use "Also" or just start the sentence.)
- **"Moreover,"** (Too formal. Use "Plus" or nothing.)
- **"In summary,"**
- **"Aims to"** (Use "Will" or "Does".)

## Business Jargon

Replace with plain language.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, address |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, embrace |
| Game-changer | Significant, important |
| Double down | Commit, increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis, examination |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |

## Filler Adverbs

Cut or replace:

- "At its core"
- "Ultimately,"
- "Interestingly,"
- "Importantly,"
- "Crucially,"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"

## Meta-Commentary & "Splainery"

- "Hint:"
- "Plot twist:"
- "Spoiler:"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Remember:" (Don't command the reader to remember. Make it memorable.)
