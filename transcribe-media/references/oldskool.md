# Oldskool Domain Reference

Read this only when the user explicitly asks for Oldskool analysis or the recording is clearly about that domain.

## Core Interpretation Rule

Oldskool planning conversations are evidence of plans, assumptions, negotiations, and operating logic. They are not accounting records, legal opinions, board resolutions, or verified commitments unless another source proves that status.

Apply this to:

- Repair/tadilat amounts.
- Alcohol and music licensing estimates.
- Revenue targets.
- Founder distributions.
- Rent-equivalent contributions.
- Sweat-equity claims.
- Grant and incentive amounts.
- Supplier or staffing costs.

## Entity Boundaries

Keep these distinct:

- Oldskool as venue/campus/operator.
- Any lab, research, product, operating, or partner entity only when explicitly mentioned in the transcript/source text.
- Founders/partners as individuals.
- Partner collectives such as Darkbloc.
- Suppliers, public bodies, artists, event organizers, and contributors.

When uncertain, write the ambiguity explicitly instead of assigning responsibility.
Do not introduce organizations, labs, or partner entities from workspace context, file paths, prior summaries, or background knowledge.

## Common Analysis Corrections

Budget phrasing:

- Weak: "1M TRY was spent."
- Better: "The meeting uses roughly 1M TRY as a repair/compliance planning anchor; verification would require receipts, invoices, bank records, signed agreements, or finance entries."

Legal phrasing:

- Weak: "BYOB is legal."
- Better: "Participants discuss whether BYOB may avoid alcohol-sales licensing, but the legality remains an open verification item."

Founder economics:

- Weak: "Profit will be split 33 percent."
- Better: "A 33 percent operating/founder share is discussed, but the base, timing, costs deducted first, and relation to unpaid labor/rent-equivalent contributions remain unresolved."

Entity boundary:

- Weak: "A lab/entity pays for landscaping."
- Better: "The source discusses whether landscape/nursery costs belong to a named entity, Oldskool, or a shared arrangement; the agreement boundary is unresolved."

## Reconciliation Priorities

When comparing old analysis docs against transcripts:

1. State the source hierarchy: transcript/media beats prior summary; finance/legal records beat conversation claims for verified amounts.
2. Identify over-weighted interpretations.
3. Preserve conflicting claims rather than smoothing them away.
4. Separate six-month triage, broader operating runway, and long-term capex.
5. Convert evidence into a plan only after marking assumptions and verification gaps.
